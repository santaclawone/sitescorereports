Mobile friendliness

A mobile-friendly website works well on smartphones and tablets. Text is readable without zooming, buttons are tappable, and content fits the screen width.

Why mobile matters

Google indexes and ranks pages based on the mobile version of your site (mobile-first indexing). If your mobile site is slow, broken, or incomplete, your rankings suffer — even if your desktop site is flawless.

For Irish small businesses, mobile is often the first interaction a potential customer has with your brand. Someone searching for a plumber, restaurant, or shop on their phone. If your site loads slowly or displays poorly, they move to a competitor.

What makes a site mobile-friendly

Responsive design — The same HTML serves all devices. CSS media queries adjust the layout for different screen sizes. No separate mobile site required.

Readable text — Font sizes should be at least 16px to prevent zooming. Line lengths of 40-60 characters per line improve readability on small screens.

Adequate touch targets — Buttons and links should be at least 48x48 pixels with sufficient spacing between them.

No horizontal scrolling — Content should fit the viewport width without requiring side-to-side scrolling.

Fast load times — Mobile networks are slower than wired connections. Optimise for speed as a core feature of your mobile experience.

Mobile usability testing

Google Search Console's Mobile Usability report identifies specific issues. Chrome DevTools Device Mode simulates various phone and tablet screens. Real device testing catches issues that emulators miss.
